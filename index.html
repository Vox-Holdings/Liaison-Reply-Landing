Example · Relationship
Step 1 · Incoming message

“Wow. Okay. So you saw my message at 9 and just decided not to answer. Good to know where I stand.”

Step 2 · The read
Strategic coaching

This is hurt dressed as a verdict. They have already decided what your silence meant, so a bare excuse confirms it. The move is to own the miss without over-explaining, and reopen the conversation tonight.

  • • Do not lead with the reason. Lead with the miss.
  • • End on an invitation, not a defense.
Step 3 · Three ways to answer
De-escalate

“You’re right to call that out. I saw it while driving, and it slipped my mind. That’s on me. Can we reset tonight?”

Connect

“You do matter to me. I hate that you felt ignored today. Work swallowed me and I shut down. Tell me what you needed. I’m here now.”

Hold your ground

“I get that you’re frustrated, but I can’t always answer the minute a message lands. I’m not okay with being scored on response time.”

Step 4 · Edit, with coaching
Editing · De-escalate, one sentence added

“You’re right to call that out. I saw it while driving, and it slipped my mind. That’s on me. I can’t be on my phone every second, though. Can we reset tonight?”

Warning

Strategic coaching

The added sentence turns an apology into an argument about phone rules.

  • • It answers the accusation instead of the hurt underneath it.
  • • It invites a reply about response times, not the reset you asked for.

Phrase notes

“I can’t be on my phone every second, though.” Warning

It can be read as a counter-accusation, as if they were wrong to expect a reply.

Try: Consider removing this sentence entirely. The draft already owns the miss, and the invitation lands better without the defense.

What it does, and what it doesn’t

What it does

  • Reads the whole conversation from a screenshot or pasted text, and drafts three reply options.
  • Strategic coaching while you edit, flagging wording that could land wrong.
  • Polishes your own draft when you have the words but not the wording.
  • Tunes the drafts to the relationship context and the writing persona you select.
  • Your threads are private to you. Only you can read, edit, or delete them.
  • Your first 15 generations are free. The app shows your remaining credits live.

What it doesn’t do

  • It never sends for you. You copy the reply you pick and send it yourself.
  • It reads nothing until you share a screenshot or paste the text in.
  • It never asks for access to your inbox, your contacts, or your messaging accounts.
  • Not a chatbot. A drafting tool for messages you already received.

Memory

The conversation does not reset.

Every thread keeps its history, so drafts know what was said last week, not just the last message.

  • Full thread memory. Your conversations stay whole in History, ready to pick up where you left off.
  • Pro threads are never summarized down. On Pro, the full history stays intact however long the thread runs, so nothing gets flattened into a summary.
  • Linked threads. Some situations span conversations: the same problem with your partner, your manager, your client. Link the threads and Pro drafts draw on all of them.
