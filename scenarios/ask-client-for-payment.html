Overdue invoices

How to ask a client for payment politely and get paid without a standoff.

Short answer: send a short, friendly reminder the day after the due date with the invoice number, amount and a payment link. Assume it slipped through. Follow up on a set schedule, get firmer each time, and ask for a specific payment date.

01 Copy and send

Payment reminder emails you can copy

A polite payment reminder email is specific: invoice number, amount, due date and how to pay, all in the first few lines. Each template below matches a stage of the follow-up. Fill in the [brackets] and send from your own inbox.

Friendly first reminder

One to three days after the due date.

Subject: Invoice #[number] due [date] Hi [Name], A quick note that invoice #[number] for [amount] was due on [date]. I've attached it again, and you can pay here: [link]. Could you let me know when it's scheduled? Thanks so much. [Your name]

Second reminder, a week later

Still friendly, now asking for a date.

Subject: Following up on invoice #[number] Hi [Name], Following up on invoice #[number] for [amount], now [7] days past due. Could you confirm the date it will be paid? If anything is holding it up on your side, tell me and I'll help sort it out. [Your name]

Firm overdue invoice email

Two to three weeks late. Clear, not angry.

Subject: Overdue: invoice #[number] Hi [Name], Invoice #[number] for [amount] is now [21] days overdue. Please arrange payment by [date]. Per our agreement, [late fee terms] apply after that date, and I'll need to pause new work until the account is current. Happy to jump on a call if something has changed. [Your name]

Final notice before next steps

Only after earlier reminders went unanswered.

Subject: Final notice: invoice #[number] Hi [Name], This is a final reminder that invoice #[number] for [amount] remains unpaid after [number] reminders. If payment isn't received by [date], I'll [next step from your contract, such as passing it to collections]. I'd much rather settle this directly. Reply today and we can agree a plan. [Your name]

Payment reminder text message

Only for clients you already text. Email remains the record.

Hi [Name], quick nudge: invoice #[number] for [amount] was due [date]. I just resent it by email with the payment link. Could you let me know when it's going through? Thanks!

Payment reminder subject lines

  • Invoice #[number] due [date]
  • Following up on invoice #[number]
  • Overdue: invoice #[number] for [amount]
  • Can you confirm a payment date?
  • Final notice: invoice #[number]

Put the invoice number in the subject. It makes the email easy to forward to whoever actually pays.

A fixed rhythm keeps the reminders impersonal, so nobody has to decide when it is okay to ask again. Adjust it to your terms. If your contract sets late fees or notice, follow the contract.

03 When the client stalls

What to say when a client disputes an invoice or delays payment

You sent a polite reminder. Marcus replied with a quality complaint, a budget squeeze and a request to wait. Bundled together, they make it easy to give in on all three. Here is how to pull them apart.

He hasn't refused to pay. He's testing whether the number moves.

A vague complaint after the invoice arrives is a negotiating position. The calm reply asks for specifics, separates the fix from the payment, and pins down a date.
  • “Needed a lot of fixing on our end.”Vague on purpose. Ask which pages and what changed, in writing.
  • “Tightening vendor spend this quarter.”Their budget problem, not a fault in your work. Don't discount for it.
  • “Revisit this in a few weeks.”An open-ended delay. Answer with a specific date.

Who you’re replying to

Marcus, a client. Invoice #1042 for $2,400 is overdue. He says the last pages needed fixes and wants to revisit and adjust the amount later. I want to stay professional, find out what the real issue is and get a payment date.

Direction Get clarity

Safe reply

Thanks for telling me, Marcus. I want to understand the issue. Could you send me the pages and what needed changing? I'll review them this week. In the meantime, the invoice reflects the approved work, so could you confirm when it's scheduled?

Why this works: it takes the complaint seriously while keeping the approved work and the payment as two separate conversations.

Warm reply

I'm sorry to hear the last batch made extra work for your team. That's not what I want for you. Can you send me the specific pages? If something is outside what we approved, I'll fix it at no charge. The invoice itself was due on the 1st, so could we agree on payment by the 15th?

Why this works: offering to fix real problems shows good faith, and the closing question turns an open delay into a date.

Direct reply

I'm glad to look at any specific issues. Please send the page list. The invoice covers work you approved on the 20th and was due on the 1st, so I can't hold it for a few weeks. Can you confirm payment by Friday the 15th?

Why this works: it names the approval date, declines the delay plainly, and ends on a question he has to answer.

Not sure which to send? If Marcus has paid on time before, start with Warm. If this is the third delay, Direct protects your cash flow. Safe works when you need time to check the pages first.

Other ways clients delay payment, and what to say

Payment excuses tend to repeat. Here are short replies to the common ones.

  • “It's with accounts payable.”

    Thanks. Could you share the AP contact or the expected payment date so I can follow up there directly?

  • “We pay on 60-day terms.”

    Our agreement says [30] days. I'm happy to talk about terms for future work, but this invoice is due under the current ones.

  • “Can you split it into installments?”

    I can do two payments: half by [date] and the rest by [date]. Does that work?

  • “We never received the invoice.”

    No problem. I've resent it just now, with the payment link. Could you confirm it arrived?

  • “We'll pay when the next phase starts.”

    I'd like to keep the phases separate. This invoice covers finished work, so I'll need it settled before we kick off the next one.

What not to say when you ask for payment

Anxious edits tend to give away the money before the client even asks. This edit of the Warm reply shows three lines strategic coaching flags, and what each one signals to Marcus.

Also leave out

  • Threats in the first reminder. Collections language on day three burns the relationship for no gain.
  • Guilt about your own bills. Your rent is real, but it makes the email about you instead of the invoice.
  • A pile of CCs. Copy their manager only after direct reminders fail, and say why.
  • Starting new work while it is unpaid. Every new deliverable makes the overdue one easier to ignore.

06 FAQ

Asking for payment, answered

How do you politely ask a client for payment?

Send a short email the day after the due date with the invoice number, amount, due date and a payment link. Assume it was an oversight, keep the tone friendly and ask when it will be scheduled.

What do you say in a payment reminder email?

Four things: the invoice number, the amount, the original due date and how to pay. Add a question that asks for a specific payment date, so the reply has to be concrete.

How long should you wait before following up on an unpaid invoice?

A common rhythm is a friendly reminder one to three days after the due date, a second about a week later, and a firmer notice at two to three weeks.

How do you ask for payment without sounding rude?

Stick to facts and skip apologies. A neutral subject line, the invoice details and a clear request read as professional, not pushy.

What do you do if a client says the work wasn't good enough after you invoice?

Ask for the specific problems in writing, compare them to what was approved, and fix genuine issues. Keep the payment for approved work as a separate conversation with a date.

Should you charge a late fee on an overdue invoice?

Only if your contract or invoice terms include one. If they do, mention it in the firm reminder, not the first friendly one.

Can you text a client about an overdue payment?

If you already text that client, a short nudge is fine. Follow it with an email so the reminder is on record.

When should you stop working for a client who hasn't paid?

Pausing new work once an invoice is two to three weeks overdue is common and reasonable. Tell them in writing, calmly, before you pause.

What is a polite subject line for a payment reminder?

Use the invoice number and date, such as “Invoice #1042 due March 1”. It is easy to find and easy to forward to accounts payable.
