How to tell a client a project is delayed early, honestly, with a new date.

Short answer: tell them as soon as you know, before the deadline passes. Say what is late, give a short reason without excuses, share a realistic new date, and say what they'll get in the meantime. One clear email beats several vague updates.

01 Copy and send

Project delay emails to copy

A good project delay email answers the client's next three questions before they ask: what's late, when it will be ready, and what happens now. Pick the template for your situation and fill in the [brackets].

Early delay notice

As soon as you know the date will move.

Subject: Update on [project] timeline Hi [Name], I want to flag early that [deliverable] will be [one week] later than planned. [Short reason, one sentence]. The new date is [date]. In the meantime, I'll send [partial deliverable] on [date] so you can keep moving. Happy to talk it through. [Your name]

Delay caused by the client

Waiting on their content or feedback. No blame.

Subject: [Project] timeline Hi [Name], Quick update: I'm still waiting on [content / approval] from your side, which was due [date]. To hit the launch, I'd need it by [date]. If that's tight, the new delivery date would be [date]. Which works better for you? [Your name]

Delay with options

Let them choose between scope and date.

Hi [Name], [Deliverable] is running behind. I see two options: 1. Launch on the original date with [reduced scope], and add [feature] by [date]. 2. Launch everything together on [new date]. Let me know which you'd prefer and I'll plan around it.

Second delay, owning it

When the date has already moved once.

Hi [Name], I'm sorry to do this again. [Deliverable] needs [three more days]. I misjudged [part], and that's on me. I'll deliver by [date], and I'll send a progress check on [date] so there are no more surprises.

Short delay text message

Only for clients you already message.

Hi [Name], heads up: [deliverable] will be with you [Thursday] instead of Tuesday. Everything else is on track. I'll email the details now.

03 When they push back

How to handle a client upset about a delay

Tom is under pressure from his board, and he's asking two fair questions: what happened, and why should he trust the new date. Long excuses won't help him. Facts, a plan and something to show his board will.

He needs a story he can tell upstairs. Give him one.

When a client is embarrassed by a delay, help them look good. Give a clear reason, a believable date and something concrete to share.
  • “We've already told our board.”His real problem. Offer something he can show on the 7th.
  • “What exactly happened?”A fair question. One or two plain sentences.
  • “How do I know the 14th won't slip?”Answer with checkpoints, not promises.

The payment integration needed extra verification steps. That work is finished. The 14th includes two days of buffer. I'll confirm on the 10th that we're on track, and you'll have screens on Friday.

Why this works: short, factual and confident, with a buffer that makes the date believable.

Not sure which to send? Warm is best when the client has a meeting to survive. Direct suits technical clients who want facts. Safe works for everything in between.

Other client reactions to a delay

Short, calm answers for the follow up messages.

  • “Can you just work the weekend?”

    I've built in the fastest realistic timeline. Rushing would risk bugs in the payment flow, which would cost more time later.

  • “We need a discount for this.”

    I understand the frustration. Let's get the prototype delivered well first, then I'm happy to talk about it.

  • “Why didn't you tell us sooner?”

    Fair point. I should have flagged the risk last week. From now on I'll send a short update every Friday.

  • “Can we cut features instead?”

    Yes. If we drop [feature] from this round, I can hit the 7th. Want me to do that?

  • “Okay, thanks for the heads up.”

    Thanks for understanding, Tom. Screens coming Friday.

What not to say when a project is late

Defensive detail and vague promises make delay emails worse. This edit of the Warm reply gets three lines flagged by live coaching, and why each one costs trust.

Live coachingWarning

Your edit of the Warm reply

It's not my fault. It should hopefully be done soon, probably the 14th but no promises.

Phrase notes

  • It's not my faultFault is not his question, so it sounds like you care more about blame than the project.Try: Here's where things stand
  • It should hopefully be done soonHope is not a date, so give a specific one.Try: It will be ready on the 14th
  • probably the 14th but no promisesIt undercuts the new date in the same sentence.Try: and I'll confirm progress on Monday

Also leave out

  • Waiting until the deadline day. The later the notice, the bigger the damage.
  • A long technical explanation. Offer more detail on request.
  • A new date with no buffer. A second slip hurts far more than the first.
  • Going quiet until it is done. Silence feels like a second delay.

06 FAQ

Project delay emails, answered

How do you tell a client a project is delayed?

Tell them as soon as you know, in one clear email: what is late, a short reason, a specific new date and what they'll get in the meantime.

How do you write a delay email to a client?

Keep it short. Use a subject like “Update on [project] timeline”, lead with the new date, give one sentence of reason and close with the next check in.

How do you apologize to a client for a delay?

A brief, sincere apology for the impact is enough: “I'm sorry for the change of plans.” Then focus on the new date and the plan.

Should you explain why a project is late?

Yes, briefly. One or two plain sentences show you understand the cause. Long technical detail sounds like an excuse.

What if the delay is the client's fault?

State the dependency neutrally, such as waiting on content due on a date, and give them a choice: send it by a date, or accept a new delivery date.

How do you rebuild trust after a missed deadline?

Give a date with buffer, add checkpoints before it, and then hit them. Small kept promises rebuild trust faster than big new ones.

How do you tell a client about a second delay?

Own it plainly, explain what you misjudged, give a firm date, and add a progress check so there are no further surprises.

Can you tell a client about a delay by text?

A short heads up text is fine if you usually text that client, followed by an email with the details for the record.
